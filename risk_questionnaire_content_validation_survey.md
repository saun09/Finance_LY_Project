# Content Validation Survey — Reviewer Questions

For step 4 of the validation plan: at least 5 reviewers, each rating every item for relevance, clarity, and whether the answer choices are correctly ordered from lower to higher risk. Copy the sections below into Google Forms (or similar) as-is — one "Section" per item works well if the form tool supports sections.

---

## Intro text (top of form)

> **Reviewing an investor risk-profile questionnaire**
>
> This questionnaire is part of a personal finance app. It combines two kinds of items: a **risk-tolerance core** (4 items) that measures how psychologically willing someone is to take on investment risk, and three **contextual profile items** — investment horizon, investment experience, and investment goal — that are commonly collected alongside risk tolerance but measure related, separate things. (For example, more investment experience is not assumed to mean higher risk tolerance — it's scored as its own dimension.) Each item below is labeled with which of these it's meant to measure. None of this assesses a person's financial *ability* to take risk (income, expenses, debt) — that's calculated separately, elsewhere, from actual financial data, and isn't asked here.
>
> Below are 7 questions, each with 5 answer choices. For each question, you'll be asked to rate its relevance to the dimension it's meant to measure, how clearly it's worded, and whether the five answer choices are genuinely ordered from lowest to highest on that dimension. There are no right or wrong answers here — I'm asking for your judgment as a reviewer, not testing your own risk tolerance.
>
> Takes about 10–15 minutes.

## Reviewer background (once, at the start)

1. Name (optional)
2. Your professional designation/role (e.g. financial advisor, CFA charterholder, psychometrician/survey methodologist, academic researcher, other — please specify)
3. Approximately how many years of relevant experience do you have in that role?
4. Do you have professional experience in finance, investing, or psychometrics/survey design? (Yes / No / Some — free text if useful)

## Per-item block (repeat for each of the 7 items below)

Q1 and Q2 use a 4-point forced-choice scale (no neutral midpoint) so that an item-level content validity index (I-CVI) can be calculated as the proportion of reviewers rating each item 3 or 4. For every item, ask exactly these questions:

**Q1 — Relevance** (4-point scale: 1 = Not relevant / 2 = Somewhat relevant, needs major revision / 3 = Relevant, needs minor revision / 4 = Highly relevant)
> "This question is relevant to measuring **[construct noted for this item]**."

**Q2 — Clarity** (4-point scale: 1 = Not clear / 2 = Somewhat clear, needs major revision / 3 = Clear, needs minor revision / 4 = Very clear)
> "This question's wording is clear and unambiguous."

**Q3 — Answer ordering** (multiple choice: Yes / No / Unsure)
> "The five answer choices are genuinely ordered from lowest to highest on the dimension this item measures."

**Q4 — Comment** (open text, optional; shown after every item)
> "Add any comments here — especially if you rated relevance or clarity below 4, or answered No/Unsure to Q3. Briefly explain why."

### Item 6 — Sure-gain tradeoff

**Construct:** Risk tolerance (core)

**Question shown to reviewer:** "Which would you choose: a guaranteed amount, or a chance at more?"
**Answer choices (shown in this order):**
1. A guaranteed Rs 5,000
2. A 70% chance at Rs 7,000, otherwise nothing
3. A 50% chance at Rs 10,000, otherwise nothing
4. A 30% chance at Rs 17,000, otherwise nothing

*Note for reviewers: the expected value of each option is designed to stay roughly constant (~Rs 5,000) across all five choices (5000 → 4900 → 5000 → 5100 → 5000). ## Closing questions (once, at the end)

1. (Open text) "Taken as a whole, does this set of 7 questions adequately cover what it's meant to (risk tolerance plus horizon/experience/goal)? Is anything important missing, or does anything feel redundant with another item?"
2. (Open text) "Any question you think should be reworded, removed, or replaced entirely? Please specify which one and why."

## Scoring this survey once responses are in

- **Relevance / clarity (I-CVI)**: for each item, compute the proportion of reviewers who rated it 3 or 4 (out of the 4-point scale) on Q1 and Q2, separately. A common threshold is I-CVI ≥ 0.78 (Lynn, 1986; Polit & Beck, 2006) for an item to be considered acceptable as-is; items below that should be revised or dropped, drawing on the Q4 comments. Averaging item-level I-CVIs gives the scale-level S-CVI.
- **Answer ordering (content validity index)**: for each item, the proportion of reviewers who answered "Yes" on Q3. The same ≥0.78–0.80 threshold applies; anything below that should be revised based on the Q4 comments before the pilot.
- **Item 7 wording check**: report separately — the proportion of reviewers who say "a real gambler" should be reworded, plus their suggested alternatives, before deciding whether to change it.
